Part 3 · Design system

Calm luxury, like an heirloom diary

Deep charcoal at night and warm ivory by day, with gold reserved for the moments that matter: the next event, the Share button and milestone badges. Names are set in an elegant serif and everything else in a clean, highly legible sans. Nothing flashes or competes. The countdown and the person's face lead each screen.

Rules the app follows

29 February birthdays

In non-leap years, the event falls on 28 Feb or 1 Mar, whichever you chose for that event.

Monthly on the 31st

In shorter months it falls on the last day: 30 Apr, 28 or 29 Feb.

One-time events

Count down once. Afterwards they move to a "Past" list and never repeat.

Age and years

"Turning 60" and "35 years married" appear only when a year is known. Milestones (18, 25, 50, 60, 75, 80; silver and golden anniversaries) get a badge.

Phone numbers

98450 12345, 098450 12345, +91 98450 12345 and 91-9845012345 are all saved as +919845012345. Numbers from other countries keep their own code.

Time zones

For someone abroad, "their midnight" is worked out in their city's time, including daylight saving, and then shown in yours.

Contact sync

Matched by the phone's contact ID, never by name. If you edited a number by hand, a changed contact shows a notice instead of overwriting it.

Stars never gate anything

No reminder, alarm or feature checks stars. They're used only by sorting and filters.

Festival dates · Mahalakshmi calendar system

Matches Marathi calendars

For 2024 and 2025, all 23 calculated festivals match the dates Marathi calendars printed. That includes Diwali on 1 Nov 2024 and 21 Oct 2025, where Marathi calendars differ from North Indian ones by a day.

Checked against published lists

2026 and 2027 match published festival lists. Ganesh Chaturthi, Holi and Diwali for 2028–2035 also match where published dates exist, and full-moon start and end times agree to within a minute.

One correction

Holi 2026 is set to 4 March, the day colours were actually played because of the lunar eclipse on 3 March. It's in the past, so it doesn't affect any reminders.

Please glance at your calendar

The Mahalakshmi website is blocked from my computer, so I couldn't open their printed pages directly. If you have the 2026 or 2027 calendar at home, compare a few dates. Any date can be corrected in the app or in the file.

Under the hood

Tech choices & phone permissions

Building blocks

  • Flutter, Android now, iOS-ready structure
  • Drift (SQLite) database. I picked it over Isar, which is no longer actively maintained
  • Riverpod to keep screens in sync
  • Local notifications + exact alarms for reminders
  • Automated tests for every date rule above

Permissions the app will ask for

  • Notifications and exact alarms
  • Full-screen alerts (midnight alarm on lock screen)
  • Run after restart (re-sets all alarms)
  • Phone calls (direct dialling)
  • Contacts (read only)
  • Ignore battery optimisation
  • Fingerprint (Phase 7) · Calendar (Phase 7, optional)

Each one is asked for only when first needed, with a sentence explaining why.

Three things I'd like you to know up front
  • Festival dates follow the Mahalakshmi calendar's system (the Marathi almanac rules). Eid has been removed. See Festival dates for the full list and how it was checked.
  • Google Calendar sync: I recommend writing to the Google Calendar already on your phone. That needs no sign-in or Google developer setup and gives the same result. A direct sign-in is also possible but needs extra setup.
  • Hindi and Kannada messages: I'll write them the way people actually text. In Phase 4 I'll show you a sample of 20 to check before writing the rest.
